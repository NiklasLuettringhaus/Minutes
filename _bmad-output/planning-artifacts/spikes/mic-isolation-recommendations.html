The short version

The best fix needs nothing you don't already have, and the cheapest fix is free but only works on a Mac. The separation already succeeded — room-0, room-1 and four remote speakers were all correctly distinguished. What failed is that the note printed all of it. That is a rendering decision, not a signal-processing problem, and it is one afternoon's work.

The real fix is enrolling your voice once, which turns "how many people are in the room" into "which one is Niklas". Both primitives for that are already linked into the app.

Before anything elseWindows is not a port

Has to be said

System audio capture is built on CoreAudio process taps, which have no Windows equivalent. Transcription and diarization run on CoreML and the Neural Engine. The UI is SwiftUI with MenuBarExtra. Detection reads CoreAudio process objects.

A Windows version is a second product that shares a file format, not a recompile. Your PRD already scopes this to Apple Silicon, and I would rather say so now than have it surface as a surprise later.

What the constraint does legitimately change is the ranking below. Anything leaning on Apple-specific processing is an optimisation for one platform — never the mechanism the feature depends on. That rules out making Apple's Voice Isolation the answer, even though it is the cheapest thing here.

The three-channel trap

With voice processing on, the input reports three deinterleaved channels. Our writer downmixes to mono by averaging — so if one channel is the processed voice and the others carry the raw or reference signal, averaging would re-add the noise the unit just removed. Which channel is which was not established: that needs a controlled recording with a known interfering source, and you were mid-meeting. No voice-processing change should ship before that measurement.

Fourteen utterances in that note are attributed to you and are not verified to be you. When more than one voice shares the mic, every matched span becomes room-N — but an unmatched one falls through to a default of local, which renders as "Me":

Why enrolment is the real answer

It is the only technique that answers which voice is yours rather than how many voices there are — and it is the only one that behaves the same on a borrowed MacBook, a headset, or a future non-Apple build. Thirty seconds of you reading a sentence, one stored profile, one comparison at attribution time. SpeakerDirectory already stores centroids and does cosine matching; SpeakerKit already exposes the comparison.

Your PRD deferred "voiceprint enrolment" to v2, reasoning that profiles are learned passively from renames. That holds for other people and fails for you: passive learning needs a correct label to learn from, and when several voices share one mic there is no reliable label to start from. Enrolling one person is a different requirement from enrolling everyone, and it shouldn't have been deferred with it.

Your questionWhat you should be able to change

The principle I'd apply: expose outcomes, hide mechanisms. You can reasonably decide what appears in your own notes. Nobody — including you — should be tuning a cosine distance.

Configurable

outcomes you own

  • Whether in-room voices appear in the note — with a per-meeting override. It's an editorial decision about your document.
  • Voice enrolment — opt-in, re-recordable, deletable. Biometric-adjacent, so §9.1 governs it.
  • The mic mute — already shipped.
  • Apple voice processing — one switch, default off until measured, worded as an effect rather than an audio-unit name.

Not configurable

mechanisms that would only break

  • The speaker-matching threshold. The number most likely to be set wrongly and hardest to reason about — and a wrong value puts the wrong name on someone's words. Calibrate it, ship one value, and expose corrections instead, which the voices list already does.
  • VAD parameters, level thresholds, cluster counts, which processed channel to read.
  • Whether diarization runs. Load-bearing for attribution.
  • Anything that disables the honesty guarantees. A room voice may be left out of the note, but it must stay visible in the app, and the note must record that speech was excluded.

Deliberately not offered at all

tempting, and wrong

  • Auto-muting when the far end speaks. It would have helped in this exact meeting, and it fails precisely when you are the one talking.
  • A "clean up my audio" master switch. It bundles techniques with different portability and different failure modes behind one control nobody could reason about — including whoever has to debug it.

RecommendationWhat I'd do, in order

  • Now, together: options 1, 3 and 4, plus the mislabel fix. All small, all independent, and 4 makes every later measurement interpretable instead of guesswork.
  • Next: option 2. The real fix, and the one that survives a change of machine.
  • Its own spike, with a controlled recording: option 5. Promising, and the only one that could remove the neighbour before it reaches disk — but the channel question has to be settled first.
  • Hold: 6 and 7, unless 2 proves insufficient.

One non-code note worth more than most of the above: a headset mic sits centimetres from your mouth and metres from your neighbour, and no amount of processing beats that geometry. You were on AirPods Pro, which is already the good case — with Voice Isolation switched on it would likely have been a different transcript.
